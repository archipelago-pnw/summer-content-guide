Alaskeros / The Cable Layers
6:00 PM
Honors the crew of Filipino men aboard the USAT Burnside who laid copper telegram cable along the Alaskan coast — unnamed laborers who built the infrastructure of global communication. When they arrived in Sitka they could message 800 miles: "honey, I'll be home soon."
Smoked miki noodles · geoduck kinilaw · rockfish kinilaw · wild rice & porcini · fermented brassica dressing · snap pea mint purée · parsley oil powder

13" E-Ink Frame

Filipino Community Hall (center) on Franklin St, Juneau Downtown Historic District, Southeast Alaska — a gathering place for Filipino cannery and cable workers in the region.

Slide · Personal Viewer

The CS Burnside: built 1882, sold to a Spanish company in 1891 and renamed Rita, captured by the US during the Spanish-American War and renamed Burnside. Converted into a cable ship while laying cable in the Philippines. In 1903 began work on the US-Alaska cable — 291nm Sitka to Juneau, then 1,070nm Sitka to Seattle, and 640nm Sitka to Valdez. Operated by the US Army Signals Corps for the Washington-Alaska Military Cable and Telegraph System (WAMCATS).

Mahabang Buhay / Seattle's Hooverville
6:30 PM
During the Depression, Filipinos were pushed out of universities and jobs. Just south of the Chinatown International District stood Seattle's largest Hooverville — home to over 1,000 men, many Filipino. They cooked with offal and discards from nearby slaughterhouses, feeding their neighbors with skill and generosity.

Tradition / Local 7 Beginnings
6:45 PM
From canneries in Alaska to farms in Yakima, Filipino workers faced riots and violence during the Depression. They organized in coalition — and Local 7, Filipino-American led, was formed uniting Cannery Workers and Farm Workers under representation for the first time.
Pork belly & pork loin · shiitake · mang tomas · herbed green purée (lemonbalm, tarragon, parsley, mint) · heirloom corn cream · spot prawn oil · carrot & bell pepper escabeche · fermented brassica dressing · maple confit · huckleberry gold potato & albacore tinapa filling
9" E-Ink Frame

Group photo includes many influential Filipino Local 7 leaders, including Chris Mensalvas. Born 1909 in San Manuel, Pangasinan, Philippines, Mensalvas migrated to the US in 1927. He attended UCLA to study law, went to work in the farms and began organizing — subject to violent and racist attacks in Wapato and Kent, WA. He worked closely with author Carlos Bulosan.

13" E-Ink Frame

Historical photograph: Executive Board elected at the 1st National Convention of the United Cannery, Agricultural, Packing and Allied Workers of America (UCAPAWA) — Denver, Colorado, July 9–12, 1937. ~25 men in front of a banner reading "Build a Strong International Union." This convention formalized the Filipino-led labor coalition that became Local 7.

Tailor Made / Two Marias
7:15 PM
Two women named Maria. Maria Luisa Dominguez — granddaughter of a Filipino revolutionary, double agent during WWII, saved dozens of prisoners while framed as a Japanese propagandist. Signed her secret letters: "remember pal, I'm quite a handful." Maria Orosa — food chemist (UW 1919), invented banana ketchup, smuggled life-saving superfoods into POW camps. Died Feb. 13, 1945 when her hospital was bombed. Two war heroes.

Panghimagas Cold / Strawberry Queen of Bainbridge
7:40 PM
Summer 1943: the Suyematsu family was forcibly interned at Manzanar just before their largest-ever harvest — their strawberries rotted. Filipino workers stepped up to tend the land. Bitter enemies in Manila, Filipinos and Japanese found solidarity on Bainbridge. For the first time, Lila Sidley Lawson, a Bainbridge Filipina, was crowned Strawberry Queen. The next year on those same cables: "today, we are free."
Strawberry compote · leche flan ice cream · whipped condensed cream · diced strawberries · snap pea pods · pumpkin seed crumble · sugar snap peas · red radish · pine-apple ice · pickled sweet onion · fried garlic · tarragon · lemonbalm (minced) · hoja santa powder · fried red onion
9" E-Ink Frame

Filipino American Community of Bainbridge — 25th anniversary group photo.

13" E-Ink Frame

Bainbridge Island Filipino Community parade float with the first Fil-Am Strawberry Queen. Caption: "Shirley Fanayon, 1st Fil-Am Strawberry Queen, Bainbridge." When the Suyematsu family and other Japanese American farmers were forcibly interned at Manzanar in March 1942 — just before their largest-ever harvest — Filipino workers tended their land. The annual Strawberry Festival, revived in 1948, continues today.

Hot Panghimagas / Domingo & Viernes
7:50 PM
Silme Domingo and Gene Viernes worked like their fathers in the Alaskan canneries, then organized labor across the US and the Philippines. Their coalition work put targets on their backs. On June 1, 1981, both 29 years old, they were shot inside their Pioneer Square union hall by agents of the Marcos dictatorship. Their murder sparked a movement — in 1986 the Filipino people overthrew Marcos.

"Turn Anguish to Anger" march — held a few days after the murders, demanding justice for Domingo and Viernes. They were murdered in June 1981.
